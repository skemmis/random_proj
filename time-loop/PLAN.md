# The Loop Hypothesis: plan

*Written October 13, 2026, for Sam's spin-off of the Butler project.*

## The conceit

This series investigates, with a straight face, the claim that Donald Trump is trapped in a time loop like the one in the film *Groundhog Day*. Each page sets one trait of a time-loop hero beside a moment from Trump's public record and asks whether the loop explains the moment better than the ordinary explanation does. The series ends on Sam's hypothesis about the Butler shooting: that a bullet through the ear was the only way for Trump to win, so he had to relive July 13, 2024, until a bullet grazed only his ear, as Major William Cage relives the same battle in *Edge of Tomorrow*. The Butler project's own figure, 1 in 625, measures how many tries that would take.

## What the pages can and cannot show

Claude cannot embed video in a published page. The page frame blocks YouTube players, and YouTube, Rumble and C-SPAN refuse downloads from this environment. Each clip therefore appears as a looping strip of four still frames, which YouTube generates automatically for every upload, with a link that opens the clip on YouTube. If Sam wants real moving footage side by side, the route is short clips from the Internet Archive's TV News Archive (which allows downloads here) for the Trump side, published as video files beside the page. The film side would still need Sam to supply the clips himself. This is Sam's personal, non-commercial project; the pages credit every clip's uploader and claim no rights to the footage.

Claude matched each clip by its title and by looking at its four frames. Claude has not watched the clips, so a timestamp inside a long clip is not given unless it was checked.

## The films and shows

| Work | Who is looped | Why it is useful |
|---|---|---|
| *Groundhog Day* (1993, Harold Ramis) | Phil Connors (Bill Murray), a TV weatherman stuck in Punxsutawney, Pennsylvania, on February 2 | The model case. It covers every stage: disbelief, indulgence without consequences, despair, mastery. It is also set in Pennsylvania. |
| *Edge of Tomorrow* (2014, Doug Liman) | Major William Cage (Tom Cruise), who relives a failed beach invasion | Dying is the reset button, and he wins only by finding the one path that works. This is the Butler hypothesis. |
| *Palm Springs* (2020, Max Barbakow) | Nyles (Andy Samberg) and Sarah (Cristin Milioti), stuck at a wedding in the California desert | Nyles has looped so long he has stopped caring about anything, including being shot with arrows. |
| *Russian Doll* (Netflix series, 2019) | Nadia Vulvokov (Natasha Lyonne), who keeps dying on the night of her 36th birthday | Repeated deaths in absurd accidents, and a loop that ends only when she fixes something specific. |
| *Happy Death Day* (2017, Christopher Landon) | Tree Gelbman (Jessica Rothe), murdered on her birthday until she identifies the killer | The loop as a puzzle about who wants you dead. |
| *Source Code* (2011, Duncan Jones) | Captain Colter Stevens (Jake Gyllenhaal), sent back into the same eight minutes on a train | Eight-minute loops to stop a bomber: the closest film to a short, violent, repeated event. |
| *Boss Level* (2021, Joe Carnahan) | Roy Pulver (Frank Grillo), killed by assassins every morning | Being hunted by gunmen every day and learning their positions. |
| *Doctor Strange* (2016, Scott Derrickson) | Stephen Strange (Benedict Cumberbatch), who traps the villain Dormammu in a loop | "Dormammu, I've come to bargain": he wins a negotiation by being killed until the other side gives in. A natural pairing with Trump's deal-making. |
| *Star Trek: The Next Generation*, "Cause and Effect" (1992) | The crew of the Enterprise, destroyed in a collision again and again | A loop the characters do not remember, broken only by a message to their future selves. |

*Looper* (2012) is about time travel, not a loop, so the series leaves it out.

## Other pairings for later pages

- **The bargain by exhaustion.** *Doctor Strange*'s Dormammu scene ([Marvel Entertainment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DCrFkaZL254)) beside Trump's habit of outlasting the other side, for example the 35-day government shutdown of 2018 and 2019.
- **Playing the piano overnight.** Phil learns the piano and ice sculpture over many loops. A Trump pairing would need a skill he seems to have acquired suddenly; Claude has not found a strong one yet.
- **Learning everyone's secrets.** Phil uses what he learned from one loop to charm Nancy Taylor in the next. A Trump pairing would be a debate line that seems prepared for an opponent's exact words.
- **Decades of foreknowledge.** Trump told Oprah Winfrey in 1988 that he did not plan to run for president but would not rule it out ([HLN](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TzisNhRldmg)). In loop terms, he had seen how it ends.
- **The fist.** Trump rose, raised his fist and mouthed "Fight" within seconds of being shot, under a flag, in front of a photographer. A looped man would know exactly where the cameras were.

## The Butler exhibit

The closing exhibit quotes the Butler project's figures and does not recompute them. From `butler-shooting/results/RESULTS.md` and the quoted section of `results/speech_luck.json`: one head-aimed shot fired at a random moment of the speech hits only the ear 1 in 625 times (0.16%) and merely grazes him 1 in 94 times (1.06%). The only arithmetic the page adds is the chance of at least one ear-only day in N tries, 1 − (1 − 1/625)^N: even odds after about 433 days, 63% after 625, 90% after about 1,438.
