Is Donald Trump Living the Same Day Over and Over?

The heroes of time-loop films share a set of habits: they ignore the rules, fear nothing, feel no shame, master impossible feats and look bored by all of it. We set seven of those habits beside Donald Trump's public record, and then asked what a bullet through the ear in Butler, Pennsylvania, would take.

In Groundhog Day (1993), the weatherman Phil Connors wakes every morning at 6:00 to the same song on his clock radio, and every morning is February 2 again.

A time loop is a story device in which one person relives the same stretch of time again and again while everyone else experiences it only once. The looped person keeps his memories; the world resets. Films have used it for comedy, horror and war, and the people trapped in loops tend to behave in the same ways.

This page compares those behaviours with moments from Trump's public life, one trait at a time. Each exhibit pairs a scene from a time-loop film with a recorded moment from Trump's career, then sets the ordinary explanation against the loop explanation. The strips loop through four frames that YouTube takes automatically from each clip; tap a strip to pause it, or follow the link beneath it to watch the clip.

Exhibit 7 · He keeps dying until he gets it right

In Edge of Tomorrow, a soldier must die again and again until he finds the one way through. Butler may have been Trump's beach.

The hypothesis runs like this. Suppose the only version of July 13, 2024, that ends with Trump in the White House is the one in which a bullet passes through the top of his right ear and touches nothing else. Every other version ends with a miss, which changes nothing, or with a wound that kills or disables him. A man trapped in a loop would have to relive that afternoon until the bullet found his ear.

The Butler project measured how rare that outcome is. It simulated the marksmanship of the shooter, the 20-year-old gunman who fired from a roof about 135 metres away, against a 3D model of Trump's head, turning his head as the RSBN broadcast shows it turning through the six and a half minutes of his speech before the first shot.

In loop terms, 1 in 625 means Trump would have relived July 13 about 625 times on average before getting the ear-only day. Luck could make it much shorter or much longer. A graze of any kind comes much sooner, after about 65 tries at even odds, but a graze of the scalp or neck is a different wound from the one Trump got. The ear alone is what the record shows, and only about one shot in 625 produces it.

Trump's head turn at the moment the shooter fired made the odds much better than average: at his actual turn, the simulation puts an ear-only hit at about 1 in 110. Only about 6 percent of the speech found him turned that well. The loop hypothesis has an answer for that too. A man who had lived the afternoon hundreds of times would know when to look at his immigration chart.

What this page can and cannot show

The published page cannot play YouTube video, and YouTube refuses downloads from the computer that built it. Each strip therefore shows the four preview frames YouTube generates for every upload: the uploader's thumbnail and three frames taken at a quarter, half and three quarters of the way through. The frames are a sample, not the scene itself; the link under each strip opens the full clip. Several film clips are fan uploads rather than studio releases, and they may disappear.

We matched each clip by its title and its frames and checked the facts of each Trump moment against news reports, but we did not watch every clip end to end. Quotations from the films are the well-known lines of each scene.

How the Butler figures were made

The 1-in-625 and 1-in-94 figures come from the Butler project's 3D simulation, which models the shooter's aim from published marksmanship tests, Trump's head and ear from a sculpted 3D likeness fitted with an anatomical skull, and his head turn from the RSBN broadcast. A graze means the bullet touches only the ear or the outer 3 millimetres of skin. The simulation assumes the shooter aimed at the middle of Trump's head. This page adds only one calculation, the chance of at least one ear-only day in N independent tries.
